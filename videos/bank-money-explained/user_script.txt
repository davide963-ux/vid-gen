Here’s something weird about money.

When you deposit $1,000 into a bank, that exact $1,000 doesn’t just sit inside a vault waiting for you.

Banks use deposited money as part of their wider financial system.

They make loans, buy assets, process payments, and keep only enough liquid money available to handle normal withdrawals.

So technically, if every customer walked into the bank tomorrow and asked for all their money at the exact same time…

the bank probably couldn’t hand everyone physical cash immediately.

That’s basically what a bank run is.

But this doesn’t mean banks are secretly stealing your money.

Modern banks operate with liquidity requirements, capital rules, central-bank access, and deposit protection systems designed to prevent exactly that situation.

The interesting part?

Most of the money you see in your bank account isn’t physical cash at all.

It’s numbers inside a financial system.

Money is much more digital than most people realize.
